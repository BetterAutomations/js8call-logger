HARDWARE (the only thing that unlocks the rest)
- RTL-SDR with a real dongle and antenna (the plumbing is built and stubbed).
- A USB sound card with a receiver feeding it; hot-plug device names.
- Two different signals at once, which needs a second receiver.
- The SU9300: CPU profile, intervals x4, decoder cost per period.
- A clock that is genuinely wrong, for `clock --set` and a real correction.
- Offline install on the disconnected laptop.

NOT BUILT
- Band scan/hop: step 3.578 / 7.078 / 10.130 / 14.078 / 18.104 / 21.078 /
  24.922 / 28.078 MHz, score decodes per minute, keep the best. Needs an SDR we
  can retune; useless for a WebSDR tap or a line input.
- Decode sweep mid-run (it needs the cable to itself) and for a sound-card
  input (the fixture cannot be injected through a receiver).
- Clock bootstrap: apply it from the watchdog when nothing has decoded yet, and
  calibrate the ~1 s audio pipeline delay against a known transmission.
- Notification: rate-limit a fault that repeats for hours; deliver off-machine
  (mail, MQTT) through a notify_command hook.

DELIBERATELY NOT BUILT
- Continuous slew: it fights systemd-timesyncd, and moving the clock under a
  running JS8Call upsets its slot scheduler.
- Absolute date/time: cannot come from JS8 at all (frames carry sub-15 s
  phase, not a date). If that is ever needed it is a GPS puck, not this.
- GLIBC fallback build (JS8Call 2.5.2 / Qt5 / PortAudio) for distros older than
  glibc 2.38: not needed for Lubuntu 26.10.
- Flatpak: deferred until it works on hardware.
